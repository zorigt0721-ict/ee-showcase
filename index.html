What is the Extended Essay?

A deep dive into independent academic research — one of the core components of the IB Diploma Programme.

Research Question

Students formulate a clear, focused, and analytically driven research question in a subject of their choice.

Methodology

Applying appropriate research methods — from literary analysis to controlled experiments — to investigate the question.

Analysis

Critical analysis and evaluation of evidence, data, or texts to build a coherent and well-supported argument.

Reflection

Documenting the research journey through structured reflections that show intellectual growth and self-awareness.

4,000 words — a focused, in-depth piece of independent academic research.

Independent research — students choose their own topic, guided by a supervisor.

Grade A criteria — clarity of research question, rigorous analysis, logical structure, and genuine reflection.

Core component — contributes up to 3 bonus points alongside Theory of Knowledge (TOK).

Mongolian A — Language & Literature

A strong Mongolian A Extended Essay engages deeply with literary texts — whether novels, poetry, drama, or non-fiction — and explores how language, form, and cultural context shape meaning. The best essays combine close textual analysis with a clear interpretive argument rooted in Mongolian literary tradition.

Example Research Questions
RQ 1 — Literary Analysis

"How does D. Natsagdorj use imagery and symbolism in My Homeland to construct a sense of Mongolian national identity?"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: Context on Natsagdorj, significance of My Homeland, thesis statement on imagery & national identity
  2. Literary Context: Historical background of Mongolian literary nationalism in the early 20th century
  3. Analysis — Imagery: Close reading of nature imagery (steppe, sky, rivers) and its symbolic meaning
  4. Analysis — Symbolism: Examination of recurring symbols and how they construct collective identity
  5. Comparative Lens: Brief comparison with another Natsagdorj work to strengthen the argument
  6. Conclusion: How imagery and symbolism together build a vision of national identity; reflection on limitations
RQ 2 — Drama

"To what extent does the character development in B. Lkhagvasuren's The Grey Horse reflect the tension between tradition and modernity in Mongolian society?"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: Research question, context of the play, thesis on tradition vs. modernity
  2. Background: Mongolian dramatic tradition and the socio-political context of the play
  3. Analysis — Traditional Characters: How characters embody nomadic values, customs, and beliefs
  4. Analysis — Modern Characters: How opposing characters represent urbanization, change, and conflict
  5. Analysis — Resolution: How character arcs resolve or leave unresolved the tradition–modernity tension
  6. Conclusion: Evaluation of the extent of the reflection; connection to broader Mongolian cultural debate
RQ 3 — Translation

"How do English translations of S. Erdene's short stories alter the cultural nuances present in the original Mongolian texts?"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, rationale for studying translation loss, selection of 2–3 short stories
  2. Theoretical Framework: Key translation theory concepts (equivalence, domestication vs. foreignization)
  3. Analysis — Story 1: Side-by-side comparison of culturally specific language and its English rendering
  4. Analysis — Story 2: Examination of idiomatic expressions, proverbs, and tone shifts in translation
  5. Discussion: Patterns of cultural loss or adaptation across the selected texts
  6. Conclusion: Summary of how translations alter nuance; implications for cross-cultural literary exchange
RQ 4 — Poetry

"In what ways does Ch. Chimid use poetic structure and diction to explore the theme of loss in his collection Echoes of the Steppe?"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: Context on Ch. Chimid, thesis on how form and word choice convey loss
  2. Poetic Context: Overview of Mongolian poetic tradition and Chimid's place within it
  3. Analysis — Structure: Examination of stanza form, line length, enjambment, and rhythm in selected poems
  4. Analysis — Diction: Close reading of word choice, repetition, and tonal shifts related to loss
  5. Synthesis: How structure and diction work together to create emotional and thematic impact
  6. Conclusion: Evaluation of Chimid's craft; reflection on scope and limitations

✅ What makes a good Mongolian A research question?

  • Focuses on specific literary texts or a defined body of work
  • Engages with language, form, style, or cultural context
  • Allows for close textual analysis, not just summary
  • Connects literary techniques to broader themes or arguments
  • Manageable scope for 4,000 words (2–3 texts recommended)

English B — Language Acquisition

An English B Extended Essay investigates the English language in its cultural, social, or media context. The best essays go beyond description to critically analyze how language functions in real-world situations — whether in advertising, translation, digital communication, or cross-cultural encounters.

Example Research Questions
RQ 1 — Media & Language

"How do British and American news outlets differ in their use of persuasive language when reporting on climate change? (Analysis of 20 articles from 2023–2024)"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, rationale, selection criteria for the 20 articles (10 British, 10 American)
  2. Theoretical Framework: Persuasive language techniques — rhetorical appeals, framing, loaded language
  3. Methodology: Corpus selection, analytical categories, comparison method
  4. Analysis — British Outlets: Patterns in tone, word choice, and persuasive strategies
  5. Analysis — American Outlets: Patterns and comparison with British findings
  6. Discussion: Why differences exist — cultural, political, and editorial contexts
  7. Conclusion: Summary of key differences; implications for media literacy
RQ 2 — Translation

"To what extent does the English subtitle translation of the Mongolian film The Steppe preserve the cultural connotations of the original dialogue?"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, significance of subtitling for cross-cultural understanding, film overview
  2. Theoretical Framework: Audiovisual translation theory — subtitling constraints, cultural equivalence
  3. Methodology: Selection of 15–20 dialogue excerpts with cultural significance
  4. Analysis — Preserved Connotations: Examples where subtitles successfully convey cultural meaning
  5. Analysis — Lost Connotations: Examples where cultural nuance is reduced or altered
  6. Conclusion: Evaluation of preservation extent; recommendations for culturally sensitive subtitling
RQ 3 — Sociolinguistics

"How has the use of English slang on Mongolian social media platforms evolved between 2020 and 2024, and what does this reveal about language attitudes among young Mongolians?"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, context of English influence on Mongolian youth language
  2. Literature Review: Sociolinguistic theories on borrowing, code-mixing, and language attitudes
  3. Methodology: Data collection from social media posts (2020 vs. 2024 samples), categorization system
  4. Analysis — Evolution: Frequency, types, and contexts of English slang usage over the period
  5. Analysis — Attitudes: What usage patterns reveal about prestige, identity, and globalization
  6. Conclusion: Summary of trends; reflection on implications for Mongolian language and identity
RQ 4 — Culture & Identity

"In what ways does code-switching between English and Mongolian in bilingual classrooms reflect students' cultural identity? (A study of 15 students in Ulaanbaatar)"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, bilingual education context in Ulaanbaatar, significance of code-switching
  2. Theoretical Framework: Code-switching theories, cultural identity and language choice
  3. Methodology: Participant selection (n=15), observation protocol, interview design
  4. Analysis — Classroom Observations: When and why students switch languages during lessons
  5. Analysis — Interview Findings: Students' self-reported reasons and identity reflections
  6. Discussion: How code-switching patterns map to cultural identity dimensions
  7. Conclusion: Key findings on identity expression; limitations and further research directions

✅ What makes a good English B research question?

  • Investigates language in a specific cultural or social context
  • Uses a clearly defined corpus (number of texts, time period, source)
  • Goes beyond description — analyzes patterns, effects, or implications
  • Connects to broader themes (identity, globalization, power, media influence)

Business Management

A Business Management Extended Essay applies business theory and analytical tools to a real organization or market situation. The strongest essays use primary and secondary data to evaluate strategies, measure performance, or assess impact — always grounding the analysis in recognized business frameworks.

Example Research Questions
RQ 1 — Financial Performance

"To what extent has Gobi Cashmere's vertical integration strategy improved its profitability over the period 2019–2024? (Using ratio analysis of published financial statements)"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, company background, significance of vertical integration in the cashmere industry
  2. Theoretical Framework: Vertical integration theory, profitability ratios (gross margin, net margin, ROE)
  3. Methodology: Secondary data from published financial statements 2019–2024, ratio analysis approach
  4. Analysis — Financial Ratios: Year-by-year ratio calculations, trend identification, and graphical presentation
  5. Analysis — Strategic Link: Connecting ratio changes to specific integration decisions (e.g. own-retail expansion)
  6. Evaluation: Strengths and limitations of ratio analysis; external factors (COVID, commodity prices)
  7. Conclusion: Extent of profitability improvement; strategic recommendations
RQ 2 — Marketing

"How effective is social media marketing in driving customer engagement for small food and beverage businesses in Ulaanbaatar? (A survey of 80 customers and 5 businesses)"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, growth of social media marketing among Mongolian SMEs, research rationale
  2. Theoretical Framework: Customer engagement theory, AIDA model, digital marketing metrics
  3. Methodology: Survey design (n=80 customers), interview protocol (5 business owners), sampling method
  4. Analysis — Customer Perspective: Survey results on engagement behaviors (likes, shares, purchases)
  5. Analysis — Business Perspective: Owner insights on strategy, ROI, and challenges
  6. Discussion: Cross-analysis of both perspectives; effectiveness evaluation
  7. Conclusion: Key findings on effectiveness; practical recommendations for Mongolian SMEs
RQ 3 — Mergers & Acquisitions

"What was the impact of Khan Bank's acquisition of XacBank's microfinance division on its market share and customer satisfaction between 2021 and 2023?"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, overview of the acquisition, significance in Mongolian banking sector
  2. Theoretical Framework: M&A impact theory, market share analysis, customer satisfaction models
  3. Methodology: Secondary data (market reports, financial disclosures) and primary data (customer survey)
  4. Analysis — Market Share: Pre- and post-acquisition market share data, trend comparison with competitors
  5. Analysis — Customer Satisfaction: Survey findings on service quality, accessibility, and loyalty post-merger
  6. Evaluation: Synergies achieved vs. integration challenges; limitations of available data
  7. Conclusion: Overall impact assessment; lessons for future M&A in Mongolian finance
RQ 4 — Human Resources

"To what extent does employee motivation at MCS Group's retail outlets depend on non-financial incentives? (A study using Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory, n=60 employees)"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, MCS Group overview, importance of motivation in retail workforce
  2. Theoretical Framework: Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory (hygiene factors vs. motivators), comparison with Maslow
  3. Methodology: Questionnaire design based on Herzberg, sampling (n=60), Likert scale analysis
  4. Analysis — Hygiene Factors: Employee responses on salary, conditions, and job security
  5. Analysis — Motivators: Employee responses on recognition, responsibility, achievement, and growth
  6. Discussion: Relative importance of non-financial vs. financial incentives; comparison with theory
  7. Conclusion: Extent of dependence on non-financial incentives; HR recommendations for MCS Group

✅ What makes a good Business Management research question?

  • Based on a real, named organization with accessible data
  • Specifies a clear time frame and scope
  • Uses recognized business tools or frameworks (SWOT, ratio analysis, Herzberg, etc.)
  • Includes primary and/or secondary data sources
  • Avoids overly broad questions — focuses on one aspect of business performance

Biology

A Biology Extended Essay is built around a well-designed experiment or data-based investigation. The strongest essays identify clear independent and dependent variables, use appropriate controls, collect sufficient data (adequate sample size and trials), and apply statistical analysis to draw evidence-based conclusions.

Example Research Questions
RQ 1 — Plant Biology

"What is the effect of varying concentrations of NaCl solution (0%, 1%, 3%, 5%, 7%) on the germination rate of wheat (Triticum aestivum) seeds over 10 days? (n=30 seeds per group, 3 trials)"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, biological context of salt stress on germination, hypothesis
  2. Background: Literature on osmotic stress, water potential, and seed germination physiology
  3. Methodology: Experimental setup — 5 NaCl concentrations, n=30 seeds per group, 3 trials, controlled variables (temperature, light, water volume), 10-day observation period
  4. Results: Raw data tables, germination rate calculations, mean & standard deviation, graphs (bar chart / line graph)
  5. Analysis & Discussion: Statistical analysis (t-test or ANOVA), trend interpretation, comparison with published literature, explanation of biological mechanism
  6. Evaluation: Limitations (seed batch variation, sample size), systematic and random errors, suggested improvements
  7. Conclusion: Answer to RQ, extent of salt concentration effect, ecological relevance
RQ 2 — Microbiology

"To what extent do garlic extract, honey, and green tea inhibit the growth of E. coli bacteria in vitro, as measured by zone of inhibition over 48 hours? (5 replicates per treatment)"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, context on antibiotic resistance and natural antimicrobials, hypothesis
  2. Background: Literature on antimicrobial properties of garlic (allicin), honey (osmolarity, pH), and green tea (catechins)
  3. Methodology: Agar disc diffusion method, extract preparation, positive & negative controls, 5 replicates, measurement at 24h and 48h
  4. Results: Zone of inhibition data tables, mean diameter per treatment, standard deviation, photographic evidence, comparative bar graphs
  5. Analysis & Discussion: Statistical comparison across treatments, ranking of effectiveness, biological mechanisms, comparison with published MIC values
  6. Evaluation: Limitations (extract concentration standardization, single bacterial strain), reliability of replicates, ethical considerations
  7. Conclusion: Comparative effectiveness summary, implications for natural antimicrobial use
RQ 3 — Ecology

"How does distance from the Tuul River (0m, 50m, 100m, 200m) affect soil invertebrate diversity in the Bogd Khan Mountain area during September 2025? (Pitfall traps, n=5 per site)"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, ecological importance of riparian zones, hypothesis on diversity gradient
  2. Background: Literature on riparian ecology, soil moisture gradients, invertebrate biodiversity indices
  3. Methodology: Site selection (4 distances), pitfall trap design, n=5 traps per site, 72-hour collection period, species identification protocol, Simpson's Diversity Index
  4. Results: Species count per site, abundance data, diversity index calculations, data tables and graphs
  5. Analysis & Discussion: Diversity trends vs. distance, statistical significance, correlation with soil moisture, comparison with similar riparian studies
  6. Evaluation: Limitations (seasonal timing, trap bias, identification accuracy), weather variables, improvements for future study
  7. Conclusion: Relationship between river proximity and invertebrate diversity; ecological significance
RQ 4 — Human Physiology

"What is the effect of 15 minutes of aerobic exercise versus anaerobic exercise on blood glucose levels in healthy volunteers aged 16–18? (n=20 participants, measured at 0, 15, 30, and 60 minutes)"

▶ EE Structure
  1. Introduction: RQ, physiological basis of exercise and glucose metabolism, hypothesis
  2. Background: Literature on aerobic vs. anaerobic energy pathways, insulin response, glycogenolysis, and blood glucose regulation
  3. Methodology: Participant selection (n=20, ages 16–18), exercise protocols (15 min jogging vs. 15 min sprints), glucometer measurements at 0, 15, 30, 60 min, controlled variables (fasting state, time of day), ethical consent
  4. Results: Blood glucose readings per participant, mean values at each time point, standard deviation, line graphs showing glucose trends over time
  5. Analysis & Discussion: Comparison of glucose curves between exercise types, t-test at each time point, biological explanation of observed differences
  6. Evaluation: Limitations (individual metabolic variation, small sample size, glucose meter precision), ethical considerations, suggested improvements
  7. Conclusion: Comparative effect of exercise types on glucose; relevance to health and sports science

✅ What makes a good Biology research question?

  • Clearly identifies independent and dependent variables
  • Specifies measurable outcomes and units
  • Includes sample size (n=), trials, and time frame
  • Uses a realistic and ethical experimental design
  • Controls for confounding variables

What Makes a Good EE Research Question?

A universal checklist that applies across all subjects — use this to evaluate and refine your research question.

Clear & Focused

Not too broad — narrowed to a specific topic, text, time period, or case study that can be thoroughly explored.

Analytical

Not a yes/no question — uses "To what extent", "How", or "In what ways" to invite critical analysis and evaluation.

Measurable

Defines boundaries — a time period, number of texts, variables, sample size, or dataset that can be concretely analyzed.

Researchable

Data, sources, texts, or experimental setups are realistically available and accessible to the student.

Subject-Appropriate

Fits the methodology and expectations of the chosen subject — literary analysis for Mongolian A, experiments for Biology, case studies for Business.

Manageable in 4,000 Words

The scope allows for depth, not just breadth — it's better to say a lot about a little than a little about a lot.
